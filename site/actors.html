Список турецких актеров

История турецкого театра и кинематографа от зарождения в поздний османский период до триумфа золотого века Yeşilçam и современной мировой экспансии турецких сериалов (dizi).

Исторический очерк

Origins and Early Development

Turkish cinema originated in the late Ottoman period, with the first known Turkish film, Ayastefanos'taki Rus Abidesinin Yıkılışı, produced in 1914 by Fuat Uzkınay.

The Republican era saw the rise of theater-trained actors like Bedia Muvahhit, recognized as the first Turkish Muslim actress for her role in Muhsin Ertuğrul's 1923 film Ateşten Gömlek, and Cahide Sonku, who became the first major female film star in Bataklı Damın Kızı Aysel (1934).

Traditional performance forms such as Karagöz shadow theatre and meddah storytelling shaped the theatrical culture from which early cinema emerged. The establishment of Darülbedayi-i Osmani in 1914 helped professionalize acting in Istanbul.

The advent of sound in the 1930s changed the industry. Muhsin Ertuğrul's İstanbul Sokaklarında (1931), a co-production with Greece and Egypt, became the first Turkish sound film. It was followed by Bir Millet Uyanıyor (1932), which helped move Turkish cinema from stage-like adaptations toward original screen narratives.

Early Turkish actors faced censorship, limited technical infrastructure, and the difficulty of shifting from stage performance to screen acting. Before the Republic, Armenian and Greek performers had played important roles in Ottoman cinema and theatre, partly because Muslim Turkish women faced social restrictions on appearing publicly as performers.

Yeşilçam Era

The Yeşilçam era, spanning roughly from the 1950s to the 1980s, became the golden age of Turkish cinema.

Centered around Yeşilçam Street in Istanbul, the industry produced melodramas, comedies, historical adventures, rural dramas, arabesque films and social realist stories. By the mid-1960s, Turkish cinema was producing more than 200 films annually, and in the 1970s output exceeded 300 films per year.

Yeşilçam reflected Turkey's modernization problems: - rural-to-urban migration, - poverty, - class conflict, - family honor, - changing gender roles, - nostalgia for village life, - the rise of the city poor.

A distinctive practice of the period was post-synchronization dubbing. Many on-screen stars were dubbed by professional voice actors in standardized Istanbul Turkish.

Key genres included: - family melodrama, - comedy, - arabesque films, - historical epics, - village dramas, - urban migration stories.

By the late 1970s and 1980s, television and video piracy weakened the Yeşilçam studio system.

Modern Turkish Entertainment

After the 1980 military coup and the liberalization policies of the Turgut Özal era, Turkey's media environment changed significantly.

Private television began with Star TV, initially Magic Box, in 1989. This opened the way for the modern Turkish dizi industry.

By the 2000s and 2010s, Turkish television dramas became a major export product. Turkish dizis reached audiences in the Balkans, the Middle East, Central Asia, Latin America, South Asia and parts of Europe.

Major modern developments include: - the global success of Muhteşem Yüzyıl, - the rise of Netflix Turkey, - the international spread of romantic dramas, - new prestige cinema led by directors such as Nuri Bilge Ceylan, - internationally recognized actors such as Haluk Bilginer.
